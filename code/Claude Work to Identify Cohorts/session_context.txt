PROJECT
-------
Andrew Hertzberg is co-authoring an empirical economics paper studying how
medical school tuition affects physicians' Medicare billing behavior. The
paper has access to Medicare billing data for several million doctors,
including which medical school they attended and what year they graduated.
A naive regression of billing on tuition would suffer from selection bias
(schools with different tuition attract systematically different students).
The identification strategy is to construct cohorts of doctors at the same
school in nearby graduation years, where the entry-level tuition is
similar (defending common selection) but the total tuition exposure
differs (providing identifying variation).

DATA FILE
---------
File: cms-aamc-allmedschoolyears.csv (also as .xlsx) — 2,865 rows.
Columns:
  med_school    — school name (lowercase, consistent)
  grad_year     — year of graduation
  npi           — ignore (used for connecting to other datasets)
  tuition       — annual tuition in $thousands of 2014 dollars
                  (inflation-adjusted; that is why the values are not round)
  delta_tuition — year-over-year change in tuition (calculated)
  tuition_bill  — sum of tuition over the four years ending in grad_year,
                  i.e., tuition[g-3] + tuition[g-2] + tuition[g-1] + tuition[g];
                  null for the first three grad_years of each school
  prev_year     — lag of grad_year (calculated)
  skipped_year  — 0/1 dummy: 1 means there is a gap in the underlying
                  tuition series before this row

UNITS REMINDER: All tuition values are in THOUSANDS of 2014 dollars, so
37.47 = $37,470. The $2k / $4k / $7k / $10k thresholds below are all in
those same thousands.

COHORT DEFINITION RULES
-----------------------
A cohort is a set of consecutive graduating classes at the same school.
A cohort must satisfy ALL of the following:
1. Same school, with grad_years strictly consecutive (no gap years
   inside the cohort).
2. Eligibility: every member row must have a non-null tuition_bill AND
   skipped_year != 1.
3. Starting tuition (defined as tuition[grad_year - 3], i.e., the tuition
   the cohort member faced when they entered med school):
     a. Year-to-year change between consecutive cohort members <= $2k
        (this becomes redundant when the next cap is also $2k).
     b. Range across the entire cohort <= $X (currently $2k; previously $4k).
4. Range of tuition_bill across the entire cohort >= $Y (currently $10k;
   previously $7k).
5. Cohort length must be 2, 3, or 4 consecutive grad_years.
6. Cohorts must be NON-OVERLAPPING (a (school, grad_year) row belongs
   to at most one cohort).

When multiple candidate cohorts overlap, the chosen subset is selected to:
  (i)   maximize the number of non-overlapping cohorts;
  (ii)  break ties in favor of SHORTER cohorts (2 > 3 > 4);
  (iii) then in favor of LARGER tuition_bill range within the cohort;
  (iv)  then in favor of cohorts that START EARLIER in time.
This is implemented as a weighted interval-scheduling DP per school.

OUTPUT: a new column Cohort_ID. Rows in a cohort get integers 1, 2, 3, ...
counting up. Rows not in any cohort get 0. Numbering increases as you
move down the spreadsheet (alphabetical by school, then chronological
within school).
